**Answer up front.** Three humans can become two, but only by paying for it, and only after an
amendment. The reference adjudicator's removal is *lawful in disposition* (§15.1/§15.2 already carry
`REFERENCE-CONFLICT`, already excluded and published) and *unlawful in procedure* (§9.5 says a
difference **must** be adjudicated, and `reconcile` refuses to produce a row without an adjudication).
The cost is bounded and computable: the run becomes INCONCLUSIVE once the Author/Confirmer disagreement
rate `d` exceeds **8.70 %–10.00 %** depending on held-out size — *before* any allowance for staleness,
ambiguity or invalidity, which share the same budget. **One human is not enough**, for a reason that is
specific and not a matter of taste: `NOT_AVAILABLE_FROM_SOURCE` is a claim about a *search*, no
mechanical check and no published URL can verify it, and P2 (threshold 0.90) is built on it. **The
machine cannot replace an observer** in this study, and the SOURCE-COINCIDENT cap in §15.5(5) turns
that from a principle into an arithmetic obstacle.

## 1. What the adjudicator actually contributes

### 1.1 Exactly what changes in the sealed row

From `lib/reconcile.mjs`. For a disagreeing `(candidate_id, field)` the eight §9.3 value columns are
copied from a single source record `src`, and only three columns vary with the decision:

| adjudication `decision` | `src` | `resolution` | `ambiguity_flag` | `adjudicated_by` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `AUTHOR` | the Author's record | `ADJUDICATED_AUTHOR` | `none` | the adjudicator |
| `CONFIRMER` | the Confirmer's record | `ADJUDICATED_CONFIRMER` | `none` | the adjudicator |
| `REFERENCE-CONFLICT` | **still the Author's record** (`let src = a` is never reassigned) | `REFERENCE_CONFLICT` | `REFERENCE-CONFLICT` | the adjudicator |
| *(agreement)* | the Author's record | `AGREED` | `none` | `''` |

Two observations follow, one of them a defect worth reporting.

**(i) The decision is a trit, not a value.** `expected_value`, `source_class`, `source_url`,
`as_of_date` and the rest always come from one of the two already-submitted records. The adjudicator
cannot write a value: §9.5 says so, and `reconcile` gives them no column in which to write one
(`ADJUDICATION_COLUMNS` is `candidate_id, field, decision, rationale, adjudicated_by, adjudicated_at`).
The maximum information a single adjudication adds to the sealed file is therefore
**log₂ 3 ≈ 1.585 bits**, plus a free-text `rationale` that is archived but scored by nothing, plus an
identity and a date.

**(ii) [FINDING — reportable defect in the published artefact, independent of this task]** On a
`REFERENCE-CONFLICT` decision, `src` remains the **Author's** record. The sealed row therefore carries
the Author's value, source, URL and dates while `ambiguity_flag = REFERENCE-CONFLICT`. Downstream
scoring never reads those columns for a conflict row (`analysis.mjs:148` dispositions on
`ref.resolution` alone), so **no endpoint is affected**. But the reference file is published (§9.6,
§13.2), and in it every conflict row silently displays the Author's reading as though it were the
established value — an asymmetry between two records the protocol treats as symmetric and the
adjudicator saw unlabelled (§9.2.1). Suggested fix, for the owner: blank the eight value columns on a
conflict row, or carry both records explicitly. Not fixed here: `research/cpg-u1/**` is frozen and out
of my write scope.

### 1.2 So what does the choice add that the two records do not already contain?

**Informationally, almost nothing. Institutionally, the whole point.**

The two records already contain both candidate values, both sources, both search notes and both dates.
Any reader of the sealed file can see exactly what the adjudicator saw. What the two records do *not*
contain is **an answer produced by someone with no stake in it.** The adjudicator's contribution is
precisely:

1. **An uninterested selection.** The bit is generated by a third §4A-independent person who is blinded
   to recorder identity (`authorIsRecord1` randomises Record 1 / Record 2 per item from the held-out
   record hash), to CPG output, to class predictions, to the stratum, to the screening logs, to the
   agreeing records, and even to *how many* items they are looking at (§9.2.1). By construction the bit
   is uncorrelated with anything that could tilt an endpoint.
2. **Coverage.** Every `AUTHOR` or `CONFIRMER` decision returns an observation to the scored set.
   Today only the residue the adjudicator *declines to resolve* becomes `REFERENCE_CONFLICT`. Remove
   them and the recovery rate drops to zero, so the excluded share becomes `d` in full.
3. **An epistemic distinction that nothing else in the design can make.** `REFERENCE_CONFLICT` today
   means *"a disinterested third reader saw both records and could not establish the fact."* Without
   the adjudicator the same label would mean only *"two readers differed."* Those are different
   published claims. The first is about the world; the second is about the observers.

A deterministic rule can replicate (2) partially and (1) trivially (a pure function has no stake). It
cannot replicate (3) at all. That is the irreducible loss and it is not a coverage loss.

## 2. The cost, in one paragraph

Full derivation in `COVERAGE_COST_ARITHMETIC.md`. The frozen sizing rule permits **exactly 13** held-out
quota vectors (mechanically enumerated from `lib/sizing.mjs` over all 4096 permitted pilot outcomes via
`lib/frame.mjs`; `fe ∈ {12,13,14,15,17,18,20,23,26,30,36,45,60}`, N = 20…100 companies, T = 60…300
observations). §15.5(2) is implemented as `10·VALID < 9·T`, so **exactly 90 % passes** and the exclusion
budget is `floor(T/10)`. With no adjudicator and no rule, the excluded share equals `d` exactly, so:

> **Break-even: the run becomes INCONCLUSIVE as soon as `d > floor(T/10)/T`, i.e. `d_max` between
> 8.70 % (fe = 13, T = 69) and 10.00 %; the first failing rate is 10.09 %–11.67 %. At the floor
> configuration the entire budget is SIX observations out of sixty, and one disagreement is 1.67 %.**

That budget is shared with `INVALID`, `REFERENCE-STALE` and `REFERENCE-AMBIGUOUS`. **The honest
break-even is `10 % − e`**: at the floor, one company ruled inadmissible after the draw costs three
observations and halves the affordable `d` to 5 %; six non-conflict exclusions leave zero. And from
`fe = 23` upward, if disagreement concentrates on observations CPG filled, **G1 (≥ 20 fills) binds
tighter than §15.5(2)** — down to 4.39 % at `fe = 45`.

`d` itself is unmeasured. It is the decisive unknown and it can be estimated for 30 observations of
work on the development companies, which are outside held-out. **That measurement should precede the
decision.**

## 3. Which resolution rules are defensible

Full assessment in `RESOLUTION_RULE_ASSESSMENT.md`. In brief:

- **(c) prefer the more recent `as_of_date`, for `employee_count` and `revenue_range` only** —
  *defensible and strongest*. §10.2 already **defines** reference truth as "the most recent
  authoritative value" for exactly those two fields. Applying it is not resolving a disagreement; it is
  completing a definition the observers applied incompletely. Must abstain on `founded_year`, on ties,
  and on any pair involving `NOT_AVAILABLE_FROM_SOURCE` — otherwise a fresher **absence** defeats an
  older stated value and a positive silently becomes a missing.
- **(b) prefer Tier 1 over Tier 2 when the tiers differ** — *defensible, narrow*. §9.4 is frozen,
  normative, per-field and already executable (`AUTHORITY`, `authorityOf`). Must abstain on Tier 1 vs
  Tier 1 (`revenue_range` has two Tier-1 classes), on Tier 2 vs Tier 2, and on any absence. Costs the
  double-entry protection for the items it fires on, so its count must be published.
- **(a) exclude as conflict** — *principled fallback, expensive, and not bias-free*: disagreement
  correlates with fact-marginality, so excluding all of it computes **P2 on the easy cases only**, with
  an upward direction and no trace in the endpoint.
- **(d) prefer `NOT_AVAILABLE` over `STATED` or the reverse** — *reject*. Invented ordering, in neither
  §9.4 nor §10.2. Preferring `STATED` is *missing → positive* by design. Preferring `NOT_AVAILABLE` has
  an endpoint sign that depends on CPG's behaviour and therefore cannot be pre-registered.
- **(e) "conservative" — pick whichever record is less favourable to the claim under test** — *reject
  outright, circular*. "Less favourable" is definable only relative to CPG's output, so the rule cannot
  be evaluated before execution; §9.6's seal-before-execution guarantee and §9.2's blinding both
  collapse, and reference truth becomes a function of the thing it judges. **Any rule that reads CPG's
  output is circular and must be rejected.** The line is sharp: circularity comes from *what a rule
  reads*, not from *being deterministic* — (b) and (c) read only the two reference records and are not
  circular in any degree.
- **(f) round-2 re-observation by the same two people** — *recommended first step, not in the brief*.
  Reduces `d` before any exclusion rule fires, needs no third human and no new normative content, and
  attacks the dominant and most tractable cause of disagreement (transcription error: `agreementKey`
  normalises whitespace and case only, so `3682` vs `3862` is a full disagreement today). Leaks exactly
  one bit ("this item is contested"), uncorrelated with CPG.

**Legality (correcting an earlier reading).** §15.1/§15.2 already carry `REFERENCE-CONFLICT` as a
sealed, excluded, published disposition, so **no new disposition is needed**. But `REFERENCE_CONFLICT`
is not a state a disagreement falls into: it is one of three outcomes the adjudicator may **declare**
(`ADJUDICATION_DECISIONS = ['AUTHOR','CONFIRMER','REFERENCE-CONFLICT']`), and with no adjudication row
`reconcile` **errors out** — "author and confirmer disagree … independent adjudication required". So
removing the role requires a **substantive amendment to §9.1 (the role row) and §9.5 (the
reconciliation table)**, re-registration under §19.1, and a changed `reconcile` with a new instrument
aggregate. Further, §9.6 — *"`seal` refuses any reference file that is not byte-identical to the
reconciliation of the blind records"* — means **any new rule must live inside `reconcile` to be
sealable at all**; a post-processing side-car can never be sealed. The prototype is written as a
specification for an amended `reconcile`, and says so in its header.

## 4. Is one human enough for reference truth?

**No — and the reason is specific, not a matter of degree.**

§9's double entry defends against three distinct failures. They are not equally substitutable.

| Failure mode | Does one observer plus mechanical validation plus a published URL catch it? |
|---|---|
| **Transcription / mis-keying** (`3682` recorded as `3862`; `USD 1300000000` as `USD 130000000`) | **No.** `VALUE_FORMAT` accepts any four-digit year, any positive integer, any ISO-code-plus-integer. `3862` and a mistyped revenue are perfectly valid. A published URL makes the error *discoverable by a later reader* but nothing in the study detects it, and §15.2 forbids post-hoc correction of anything that would change the seal. The second observer is the only in-study detector. |
| **Single-source error** (landing on a mirror, a stale page, the wrong entity) | **Partly.** `checkValueFields` + `AUTHORITY` already refuse `AGGREGATOR`, `PRESS`, `ENCYCLOPAEDIA`, `JOB_BOARD` outright for all three fields, and `employee_count` from the entity's own page requires a stated publication date. That is real, mechanical protection and it does substitute for a second pair of eyes on *source class*. What it does not touch is **entity identity**: a single observer who resolves the company to the wrong registry entry produces a formally perfect record, and P3 (`MISATTRIBUTED-FILL`, threshold **0**) is scored against it. The decisive identifier in §9.2 mitigates but does not close this. |
| **Motivated or lazy reading — the `NOT_AVAILABLE_FROM_SOURCE` decision** | **No, and this is the decisive one.** A `NOT_AVAILABLE_FROM_SOURCE` record is a claim about a *search*: `checkValueFields` requires only a non-empty free-text `search_note`. There is no URL to publish, nothing for an outsider to re-fetch, and no possible mechanical validation of a negative. One person's "I looked and it is not there" is unfalsifiable from the record. |

The third row is why one observer fails *this* protocol specifically. **P2 — Abstention Recall,
threshold 0.90, a normative acceptance endpoint — is
`CORRECT-ABSTENTION ÷ (CORRECT-ABSTENTION + UNSUPPORTED-FILL)`, and both labels are assigned by asking
whether reference truth holds an authoritative value.** Every `CORRECT-ABSTENTION` in the numerator is
credited on the strength of a reference record that says "no authoritative value exists". With one
observer, the study's second-most-important number rests entirely on one person's search diligence,
with no check of any kind — not a URL, not a format rule, not an authority table, not an outsider.
`abstention-expected` is a whole outcome class (5–25 companies) built on exactly those records.

Note the asymmetry cleanly: **published source URLs substitute well for the second observer on `STATED`
rows and not at all on `NOT_AVAILABLE_FROM_SOURCE` rows.** A single-observer design is therefore
*conceivable* for a study whose endpoints depend only on positive values, and is **not** conceivable for
a study whose acceptance criteria include abstention recall.

**Two humans (Author + Confirmer, no adjudicator) preserve the only real check on abstention
credibility**, at the price quantified in §2. That is the design worth pursuing.

## 5. Can the machine generate reference truth?

Assessed as a *replacement for a human observer* — mechanised extraction from the §9.4 Tier 1 sources
(`REGISTRY_RECORD`, `REGULATORY_FILING`, `AUDITED_FINANCIAL_FILING`).

**What it would genuinely be better at.** Consistency (no mis-keying), completeness of audit trail
(source, fetch time, parse path all logged), reproducibility (§14 Rung 1), and *auditability* — the
extractor's source can be published and read, whereas a human's search cannot. Against §20.23
("personnel independence rests on declarations and organisational confirmation, not on verifiable
proof"), a published extractor is in one narrow respect *more* inspectable than a person. That point
should be conceded plainly rather than argued away.

### 5.1 Where circularity enters — and whether "not the resolver under test" saves it

It does not. It removes only the trivial form (a function judging itself) and leaves three channels,
each of which is enough on its own:

1. **Shared code.** §10.2's own rationale cites `wikidataAdapter.ts:172` for CPG's currency handling.
   If an extractor reuses *any* parsing, normalisation, currency, unit or entity-resolution code — or
   any library, or any regex that was written once and copied — a shared bug produces the *same wrong
   value* on both sides, and the observation scores `SUPPORTED-FILL`. §9.8's SOURCE-COINCIDENT check
   detects shared **documents** (`ref.source_url` against `cpg_source_urls`). **There is no
   CODE-COINCIDENT check anywhere in the protocol or the instrument.** This is a genuine gap and it is
   *created* by mechanising reference truth; it does not exist while humans read the pages.
2. **Shared source-selection policy.** If the extractor's notion of "the decisive registry path for
   this company" is the same notion CPG uses, then every case where CPG resolves to the wrong entity is
   a case where the reference resolves to the wrong entity too — and `MISATTRIBUTED-FILL` becomes
   *structurally unobservable*. P3's threshold is **0**, and P3 is the endpoint most directly about
   identity. Mechanising reference truth against the same registries is the one change most likely to
   make P3 untestable while leaving it apparently met.
3. **Tuning under observation.** The extractor is debugged by its authors, who can see CPG output. One
   iteration of "the extractor is wrong on this company" performed with CPG's answer visible is a §9.2
   violation with **no mechanical trace**. §9.6's seal protects against changing values *after*
   execution; it offers nothing against tuning the value-*producer* before the seal. §20.20 already
   concedes that no-preview protection is procedural and audit-based; a code artefact makes the
   temptation cheaper and the audit harder, because a commit history shows *what* changed and never
   *what the author was looking at*.

And the loss that has nothing to do with bugs: the study's central anti-circularity evidence is
*"two people who had never seen CPG wrote these facts down."* `REFERENCE_TEAM_PACKET.md` and
`reference/tools/README.md` both state this in terms as strong as English allows — "An AI substitute for
either person would destroy exactly the property being claimed." Replacing an observer with the
developing organisation's own code does not weaken that claim; it deletes it.

### 5.2 The SOURCE-COINCIDENT interaction — a second, independent objection

§9.8 / §15.5(5) [NORMATIVE]: *"If SOURCE-COINCIDENT exceeds 60 % of scored observations, the run is
INCONCLUSIVE for insufficient reference independence."* Implementation (`analysis.mjs:132-138, 202`):

```js
const coincident = (ref, urls) => {
  if (!ref.source_url) return false;
  if (D['AD-3'] === 'EXACT_URL_STRING') return urls.includes(ref.source_url);
  return urls.map(stripFragment).includes(stripFragment(ref.source_url));
};
'5_source_coincident_above_60pct': { triggered: valid.length > 0 && 10 * sc > 6 * valid.length, … }
```

**This is arguably a decisive objection to mechanised extraction, and it is independent of
circularity.** The mechanism:

- A **human** observer's URL choice is idiosyncratic. Two people looking up the same registry fact may
  cite the search-result page, the entity page, a filing PDF, a jurisdictional sub-page or a permalink.
  That variance is what keeps SOURCE-COINCIDENT below the cap even when reference and CPG consult the
  *same institution*. The cap is satisfied partly by human noise.
- A **deterministic extractor** eliminates exactly that variance. It will cite *the same canonical URL
  for every company*, by design — that is what makes it better. If CPG's grounding also resolves to
  that canonical URL, coincidence jumps from partial to near-total and §15.5(5) fires.
- **Note the perversity:** the cap punishes *more canonical, more authoritative* sourcing. The better
  the machine reference is at going straight to the Tier-1 document, the more likely the run is
  INCONCLUSIVE for "insufficient reference independence" — even though the reference is, in the
  ordinary sense, *more* reliable.
- The AD-3 decision makes this worse in one direction and no better in the other: under
  `EXACT_URL_STRING` a trailing-slash or query difference saves you by accident; under the
  fragment-stripped alternative even that accident disappears.
- **The obvious workaround is illegitimate.** One could have the extractor prefer URLs CPG is unlikely
  to cite. That is selecting sources in order to pass a validity check — gaming an independence metric
  — and it is exactly the kind of move §15.2's "exclusion rules may not change after any held-out
  result is seen" exists to forbid in spirit.

So mechanised reference extraction faces two walls: circularity it cannot escape (§5.1) and an
independence cap it makes *more* likely to fire (§5.2). §20.6 already records that "reference
independence is bounded; SOURCE-COINCIDENT is published and capped" — mechanisation spends that bounded
budget faster.

### 5.3 The one legitimate role for machine extraction

Not as an observer. As **instrumentation**, strictly outside the sealed file:

- a **third, declared, non-authoritative pass** whose only outputs are (i) a flag list of candidate
  transcription errors to send back for round-2 re-observation (§3(f)), and (ii) an estimate of `d` and
  its census on the *development* companies. It never enters `reconcile`, never enters the seal, and
  never supplies a value.
- or, if the owner wants it *as* an observer: written, hosted and run by a party satisfying §4A
  independence, with its full source and its full output published before the seal, and with an added
  **CODE-COINCIDENT declaration** (no shared parsing, normalisation, currency or entity-resolution code
  or library with the resolver under test) alongside the existing independence declarations. Its
  independence would then be exactly as strong as that declaration and no stronger — §20.23 again — and
  §5.2's SOURCE-COINCIDENT problem would remain unsolved.

**It cannot replace both observers**, and it cannot be written by the developing organisation.

## 6. What is lost by removing the reference adjudicator

Beyond coverage, five things. The first two are the serious ones.

1. **The conflict / resolvable distinction — destroyed.** See §1.2(3). Today `REFERENCE_CONFLICT` is a
   published statement about the world; without the adjudicator it degrades into a statistic about the
   observers. Mitigation: publish `CONFLICT-UNRESOLVED` as its own exclusion-table line rather than
   reusing `REFERENCE_CONFLICT`, so the published count is not inflated with un-arbitrated differences.
   This mitigates the misreporting; it does not restore the distinction.
2. **A silent upward bias on P2.** Disagreement is not independent of the *kind* of fact. The
   disagreements most likely to be `STATED` vs `NOT_AVAILABLE_FROM_SOURCE` are the marginal cases —
   exactly the observations that discriminate `CORRECT-ABSTENTION` from `UNSUPPORTED-FILL`, which are
   P2's entire numerator and denominator. Excluding all of them computes P2 on the easy cases. The
   direction is upward and it is invisible in the reported endpoint. Note carefully what is *not*
   wrong: §15.2's "No observation may be excluded because its result is unfavourable" is **not**
   violated, because the Author and Confirmer never see CPG output and so cannot aim exclusions at
   unfavourable items. The bias is a correlation with fact-marginality, not with CPG's answer. That
   makes it honest, and still real. Mitigation: publish the exclusion table split by `value_kind` pair,
   so a reader can see how much of P2's population went missing and of what kind.
3. **[FINDING] Manufactured §9.7 class-prediction mismatches.** `reconcile`'s `stratumMismatches`
   counts, per company, rows with `value_kind === 'STATED' && ambiguity_flag === 'none'`:
   ```js
   if (e.stratum === 'fill-expected' && stated === 0) stratumMismatches.push(...)
   ```
   A conflict row has `ambiguity_flag = 'REFERENCE-CONFLICT'` and is not counted. So a `fill-expected`
   company whose stated rows all become unresolved conflicts is published, under §9.7, as contradicting
   its own class prediction — when in truth nobody adjudicated it. Raising the conflict rate
   **manufactures false mismatches in the sealed, published record.** §9.7 says these are "never
   corrected by editing", so the false signal is permanent. Mitigation: exclude `CONFLICT-UNRESOLVED`
   rows from the mismatch determination, or report the mismatch as `UNDETERMINED`.
4. **A smaller α population, under one live AD-1 setting.** `AD-1` is UNDECIDED. Under
   `EXCLUDE_INVALID_CONFLICT_AND_STALE`, `REFERENCE-CONFLICT` rows are dropped from the Krippendorff
   population (`analysis.mjs:179-180`), so more conflicts means a smaller, noisier α against the 0.667
   threshold in §15.5(3) — a second, indirect route to INCONCLUSIVE. Under
   `ALL_COMPLETELY_RATED_ITEMS` this does not arise. §15.5(3)'s separate "90 % completely rated" test is
   over *all* rows and is unaffected, since raters still label conflict rows.
5. **One fewer independent person who has read the reference material.** §4A's independence structure
   is the study's defence against the developing organisation's influence. Three independent readers
   become two. Nothing measures this, and that is the point: it is a reduction in the study's human
   attack surface against the developing organisation, in exchange for a reduction in cost.

**What is *not* lost, and should be said so the trade is fair:** no value is lost (the adjudicator never
supplied one); no anti-gaming property is lost (the Author/Confirmer blinding to CPG output survives
intact, so exclusion-by-disagreement cannot be aimed); §9.6's seal-before-execution ordering survives
intact for options (a)–(c) and (f), because all of them are computable from the two records before CPG
runs.

## 7. The prototype

`prototype/resolver.mjs` — **clearly labelled PROPOSAL, not registered**, header first lines:

> `PROPOSAL - NOT REGISTERED. NOT PART OF THE INSTRUMENT. NOT A PROTOCOL CHANGE.`
> `CPG_U1_PROTOCOL_004 sha256 c50fc680...df00b719 is UNCHANGED by this file.`

It takes two **unlabelled** blind record sets (exactly §9.2.1's blinding) and emits, per
`(candidate_id, field)`, one of `AGREED` / `RESOLVED-BY-RULE(<named rule>)` / `CONFLICT-UNRESOLVED`.
Design points:

- **Never a third value.** An output-level postcondition re-checks every resolved row against both
  inputs on all eight §9.3 value columns and refuses the whole run otherwise.
- **Refuses CPG output.** A deep key scan over records, manifest and rule list rejects anything matching
  `/cpg|resolver_output|machine_state|evidence_state|grounding|company_facts|observation|pilot|instrument_check/i`,
  with `constructed_without_cpg` as the single permitted exception (it is the §9.3 attestation). Checked
  before anything else.
- **Rules are an allow-list of two**, ordered, applied first-match: `MORE_RECENT_AS_OF_TEMPORAL_FIELDS`
  then `TIER_1_OVER_TIER_2`. `PREFER_STATED_OVER_NOT_AVAILABLE` and `LESS_FAVOURABLE_TO_THE_CLAIM` are
  refused by name.
- **Order-independent.** Each rule is checked against its own mirror at run time and refuses if
  asymmetric. The reported `verdict_sha256` covers verdict + rule + chosen value and is *invariant*
  under swapping the two inputs; `chosen_side` is deliberately excluded from it, because which of two
  unlabelled sets a value came from is an artefact of presentation order and carries no
  reference-truth content.
- **Invalid is a refusal, never a verdict.** Every value rule is imported from the verified instrument
  (`checkBlindRecord`, `agreementKey`, `authorityOf`, `FIELDS`); the module adds **no value rule of its
  own**. A malformed, non-authoritative, forbidden-kind, unattested, duplicated, missing or
  same-recorder record aborts the run rather than becoming an exclusion.
- **Tamper gate.** Declared canonical record-set hashes are compared as an *unordered pair*, so swapping
  the inputs cannot turn a clean input into a tampered one.
- **Instrument gate.** Refuses any tree whose aggregate is not `f7699f05…5eb04e5cc5dd`.

**Engineering constraint, recorded:** because §9.6 makes `seal` refuse any reference file that is not
byte-identical to `reconcile`'s output, this module could never be used as a side-car. It is a
specification for an amended `reconcile`.

## 8. Recommendation

1. **Do not remove the reference adjudicator on the strength of this analysis alone.** Measure `d` first,
   by a double-entry pass over the 10 development companies (30 observations), and publish `d̂` with its
   census by `value_kind` pair, tier pair and field. The whole decision turns on a number that costs 30
   observations of human work.
2. **If `d̂` ≤ ~3 %**, a two-human design with rule (c), then rule (b), then `CONFLICT-UNRESOLVED` is
   defensible at every permitted held-out size, with headroom for staleness and invalidity.
3. **If `d̂` is 5–10 %**, add round-2 re-observation (§3(f)) *before* considering any rule; it attacks the
   cause rather than the symptom and needs no normative change.
4. **If `d̂` > 10 %**, the reference adjudicator is not a luxury — the design does not close without a
   recovery mechanism, and the only recovery mechanisms grounded in frozen text are (b) and (c), whose
   *coverage* of the disagreement space is knowable a priori but whose *share* is not.
5. **Do not mechanise reference truth**, for §5.1 and §5.2 jointly. If any machine pass is introduced,
   confine it to flagging candidate transcription errors for re-observation and to estimating `d`, and
   keep it out of `reconcile` and out of the seal.
6. **Three items for the protocol owner regardless of this task:** the conflict-row value-column
   asymmetry (§1.1(ii)), the manufactured §9.7 mismatches under a raised conflict rate (§6.3), and the
   absence of any CODE-COINCIDENT check beside §9.8's SOURCE-COINCIDENT (§5.1(1)).
