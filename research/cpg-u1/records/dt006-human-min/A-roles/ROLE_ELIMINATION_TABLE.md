## 0. Definitions used throughout (so that "can a machine replace it?" has a truth value)

Five *distinct* evidentiary properties are in play. The protocol keeps them apart and so must this analysis.

| Tag | Property | Where the protocol names it |
|---|---|---|
| **S** | **Sampling integrity** — given the committed frame, no one chose the sample. Cryptographic. | §23 "Two claims, never merged" (1); §22 rows 1–30 |
| **F** | **Selection-set unbiasedness** — the frame itself is not composed to favour the software. **Not** cryptographic. | §23 claim (2); §20.18; §22 row 31 |
| **T** | **Reference non-circularity** — the values CPG is scored against were not produced by CPG or by its developers. | §9.3 `constructed_without_cpg`; §9.8 SOURCE-COINCIDENT cap |
| **M** | **Scoring reliability** — the map from (CPG output, sealed reference) to a label is reproducible by a second party and was not chosen to favour the software. | §12 α ≥ 0.667; §15.5(3) |
| **C** | **Coverage / non-exclusion** — enough observations survive to support an endpoint. | §10.3 G1 ≥ 20; §15.5(2) ≥ 90 % VALID; §15.5(3) ≥ 90 % rated |

**Three kinds of "machine enforcement", only one of which is admissible.**

1. **Deterministic rule pinned by hash** — a total function, its source inside `tooling_aggregate_sha256`, registered per §5.5.1 **before** the frame exists and therefore before any CPG outcome is knowable (§22 rows 2, 26a). **Admissible.** Every mechanism named in this document is of this kind.
2. **A model inference (LLM/classifier) standing in for a human judgement.** **Inadmissible.** It is software of the developing organisation scoring that organisation's software (circularity C1 below), it is not byte-reproducible so it breaks §13.3 replication, and an AI is never an independent human (prior analysis X-5, class H3). No mechanism proposed here is of this kind.
3. **"Automation should suffice."** Not a mechanism. Not used.

**Three named circularity entry points**, used as the rejection test in `MODEL_RANKING.md`:

- **C1** — a reference value produced by software the developing organisation wrote. Makes `constructed_without_cpg = 'yes'` (`lib/schema.mjs:257`) machine-true but substantively false.
- **C2** — a reference value drawn from a source CPG also cites. Driven to the §9.8 cap; `lib/analysis.mjs:202` triggers INCONCLUSIVE above 60 %.
- **C3** — a **scoring rule** written after CPG behaviour was observed. **Already closed by protocol-004's own architecture**: the tooling aggregate is registered (§5.5.1) before frame construction, and the frame hash is committed before the randomness block exists (§22 row 2). A deterministic scoring rule placed in the tooling before registration is therefore **not circular**.

The asymmetry between **C1/C2 (open) and C3 (already closed)** is the single fact that makes role minimisation possible at all: *mechanising the scoring step is non-circular; mechanising the reference value is circular.*

| **INDEPENDENCE RISK** | Moderate and procedural (§20.23). Non-zero for no evidentiary gain — the worst ratio of the seven. |
| **PLACE IN A TWO-HUMAN MODEL** | **None. Delete; replace with the pinned tier rule above.** |

**Reasoning.** The decisive question is *what does this person know that the two primaries do not?* The answer, read off the instrument rather than assumed: **exactly one thing — that this item is disputed, and what the other record says.** `lib/packets.mjs:36-38` builds each item from the drawn company's public identifiers plus `pick(a, RECORD_KEYS)` and `pick(c, RECORD_KEYS)`; §9.2.1 then lists everything withheld (CPG output, class prediction, stratum, split, screening logs, agreeing records, recorder identities, **any tally**). The adjudicator receives **no source the primaries lacked** and has **no mandate to look anything up** — §9.5 forbids a third value. Therefore the adjudicator's marginal information is the *pairwise contrast* the primaries were deliberately blinded from, and nothing else.

A pairwise contrast plus a fixed authority ordering is a **function**, and §9.4 already supplies the ordering, normatively and enforced. So the human here is performing a computation the protocol has already specified. What the human adds beyond the function is **discretion** — and discretion held by a role that adds no independent source path is a liability, not an asset: it is one more declaration to trust (§20.23), one more packet-leakage surface (§22 row 35), and one more person whose non-independence would retroactively invalidate work.

One honest counter-argument, and its answer — now qualified by the exclusion budget. A tier rule cannot resolve the case where both records cite Tier 1 and disagree on the number; a human could read both filings and see that one misread a table. The *methodological* answer is that this is precisely where **exclusion is correct**: two authoritative sources in genuine conflict is what `REFERENCE_CONFLICT` means, and §10.3 requires it to be excluded from every denominator, counted and published. But the *feasibility* answer is now the binding one: each such exclusion spends one of six places at the floor. **So the honest position is not "exclusion is always better" but "exclusion is better per observation and scarce in aggregate."** This is the sharpest instance of the general trade in this analysis — the human adjudicator's real function is to keep the exclusion budget solvent, and eliminating them means either a resolution rule that keeps observations VALID, or a larger N. The second is bounded by CAP H1 = 60 (§19.2), so it must mostly be the first.

## 5. Rater 1

| Field | Finding |
|---|---|
| **CURRENT ROLE** | Applies the §10.1 decision procedure with the §10.2 agreement rules to every item of their derived-order packet: field, CPG's value or null, the §12.3 cited-URL set, the sealed reference record. |
| **REASON IT EXISTS** | §12. The scoring step must be performed by someone who did not build the software and did not choose the reference value. |
| **EVIDENTIARY PROPERTY PROTECTED** | **M — scoring reliability.** |
| **CAN MACHINE ENFORCEMENT REPLACE IT?** | **PARTIALLY — and the split is sharp: four of the five decision points are already functions; one is not.** |
| **EXACT MECHANISM (for the mechanisable part)** | Decompose §10.1's tree. **(1) "CPG returned a value?"** — already mechanical and already enforced: `lib/ratings.mjs:36-38` refuses a fill label where `cpg_value === null` and an abstention label where it is non-null; `lib/evaluation.mjs:181` sets `cpg_filled` machine-side from the archived response. **(3) "Reference holds an authoritative value?"** — a function of two sealed fields: `value_kind === 'STATED'` and `authorityOf(field, source_class) !== 'NOT_AUTHORITATIVE'` (`lib/schema.mjs:198`). **(4) "Value agrees?"** — §10.2 is arithmetic over formats §9.3 pins exactly (`VALUE_FORMAT`, `lib/schema.mjs:192`): `founded_year` is string equality of four digits; `employee_count` is `|cpg − ref| ≤ ref/10` over integers; `revenue_range` is the pinned parse (currency marker present, `$` = USD, K=10³/M=10⁶/B=10⁹, within 10 %), with the sub-reason `CURRENCY_NOT_STATED` already a typed rule (`lib/ratings.mjs:21-24`). **Temporal rule** — already mechanised outside the rater at `lib/analysis.mjs:149` (REFERENCE-STALE, cutoff from AD-5, `lib/analysis.mjs:134`). Each of these goes into the tooling aggregate before registration: entry point **C3**, already closed. |
| **IF NO, WHY NOT (for the irreducible part)** | **Branch 2 — "value attributable to the EXPECTED entity?"** — the `MISATTRIBUTED-FILL` test. No deterministic rule in the protocol or the instrument decides it, and the instrument says so in its own words: `lib/analysis.mjs:225` reports S1 as `computed: false`, reason *"company-level identity correctness needs a registry-identity comparison rule not implemented by this tooling."* Deciding it requires reading the §12.3 cited URLs (`extractCitedUrls`, `lib/raterPackets.mjs:31-49`) and judging whether the document is about the pre-registered legal entity or a same-named other. **This one branch carries the study's harshest endpoint**: P3's threshold is exactly 0 and any MISATTRIBUTED-FILL yields outright **FALSIFIED** (§10.3, §10.5; `lib/analysis.mjs:212`). `REFERENCE-AMBIGUOUS` (the judgement that the sealed reference cannot decide the item) is likewise irreducible. |

Publish α on that branch alone: a restriction of `alphaPop` (`lib/analysis.mjs:179-181`) needing no new statistic, since the 7×7 `rater_label_coincidence` matrix (`lib/analysis.mjs:182`) already carries the raw material. |
| **CONTAMINATION RISK** | Low by construction and well enforced: `checkItem` (`lib/raterPackets.mjs:54-60`) admits exactly `EVALUATION_ITEM_KEYS` — "no class, split, pilot or rater information"; §12 blinds raters to each other's labels, to tallies and to the pilot. §12.2's own caveat applies ("U1 is single-arm, so the company and the CPG output are necessarily visible"). **Combining with reference authorship adds no information and therefore no contamination**: §12's packet already contains the sealed reference record. What it adds is **self-consistency bias**, cued explicitly because `REFERENCE_COLUMNS` carries `author` / `confirmer` / `resolution` / `adjudicated_by` into the record the rater reads (`lib/schema.mjs:213-218`). **Reasoning.** Rating looks like one judgement and is five. Four are functions of fields the protocol pins exactly — and the instrument has already mechanised the first of them (`lib/ratings.mjs:36-38`) and the temporal rule (`lib/analysis.mjs:149`), which establishes the pattern rather than merely permitting it. The fifth, entity attribution, is the only place in the entire scoring path where a human reads a document and forms a view, and it is not coincidence that it is also the only place the instrument explicitly declines to compute (`lib/analysis.mjs:225`). Concentrating the human effort there is both the largest workload reduction available and the smallest possible attack surface: the item set shrinks to filled observations only, and the decision shrinks to a three-way (attributable / not / cannot tell).

## 7. Rating adjudicator

| Field | Finding |
|---|---|
| **CURRENT ROLE** | A third independent person decides each rater disagreement as `LABEL_A` / `LABEL_B` / `REFERENCE-AMBIGUOUS` on a blinded packet, **never a new label** (`lib/ratings.mjs:9`; §12.2). Mapped mechanically afterwards (`lib/packets.mjs:104-113`). |
| **REASON IT EXISTS** | §12. Without a resolution, disagreeing items have no final label — `lib/ratings.mjs:136` throws `RATING_ADJUDICATION_MISSING` — so they would count against the ≥ 90 % completely-rated rule (§15.5(3), `lib/analysis.mjs:199`) and shrink G1. |
| **EVIDENTIARY PROPERTY PROTECTED** | **C — coverage.** Nothing else. It does not improve M: it is not a third independent rating, it is a selection between two existing ones. |
| **CAN MACHINE ENFORCEMENT REPLACE IT?** | **YES.** |
| **EXACT MECHANISM** | **Pre-registered adverse resolution, plus a mandatory exclusion sensitivity.** Pin a total order over `RATING_LABELS` (`lib/packets.mjs:8`) by severity against CPG: `MISATTRIBUTED-FILL > INCORRECT-FILL > UNSUPPORTED-FILL > MISSED-FILL > CORRECT-ABSTENTION > SUPPORTED-FILL`. On a disagreement between two non-`pending` labels, the final label is the **more severe** of the two; the chosen label keeps the sub-reason its rater recorded, exactly as `lib/ratings.mjs:117` already does. The order's bytes live in the registered tooling aggregate before frame construction (**C3**, closed). **Primary analysis uses adverse resolution; a mandatory published sensitivity re-runs the analysis with every disagreement dispositioned `REFERENCE-AMBIGUOUS` (excluded).** Both are deterministic and both are recomputable by an outsider from the two sealed sheets. **Why this is evidentially stronger than the human:** adverse resolution yields an **upper bound on EFR** and **lower bounds on AR and FR** — the conservative direction for a truthfulness claim — so a SUCCESS reads "even resolving every rater disagreement against CPG, EFR ≤ 0.05." Today's human adjudicator, by contrast, resolves some disagreements *in CPG's favour*, which is exactly what a reader cannot verify. `lib/packets.mjs:81-82` (pending and agreeing items never enter adjudication) and every binding check in `finalRatings` (`lib/ratings.mjs:136-144`) are unchanged. |
| **NO LAWFUL EXCLUSION EXISTS — the asymmetry with §4** | §15.1's disposition list is **closed** — `VALID` · `INVALID` · `REFERENCE-CONFLICT` · `REFERENCE-AMBIGUOUS` · `REFERENCE-STALE` — and **none of them means "the raters disagreed."** §15.2 gives sealed `REFERENCE-CONFLICT` an explicit home, so the *reference* adjudicator can simply be deleted with disagreements falling into an existing disposition; the *rating* adjudicator cannot. The instrument confirms the gap by refusing rather than dispositioning: `lib/ratings.mjs:136` throws `RATING_ADJUDICATION_MISSING`. So removing this role needs **either** a new §15.1 disposition (say `RATER-DISAGREED`, excluded and published) **or** a deterministic resolution rule. **The exclusion budget decides between them:** a new exclusion disposition spends the six places at the floor, whereas adverse resolution keeps the observation **VALID** and spends **nothing**. Adverse resolution is therefore the recommended mechanism *and* the cheaper one — the only elimination among the five that costs no coverage at all. Mapping disagreements onto the existing `REFERENCE-AMBIGUOUS` instead is available but should be **rejected as semantically false**: that label means the sealed reference cannot decide the item, not that two people read it differently. |
| **IF NO, WHY NOT** | n/a. One consequence must be stated rather than hidden: with P3's threshold at 0 and FALSIFIED on any MISATTRIBUTED-FILL (`lib/analysis.mjs:212`), adverse resolution means **one rater's single attribution call can falsify the run.** That is a false-negative risk borne by the developer, not a false-positive risk borne by the reader. The published exclusion sensitivity is the honest way to show both. |
| **HUMAN REQUIRED?** | **NO.** |
| **COMBINABLE — TEXTUALLY PERMITTED** | **None** (§4A.3; §12 Eligibility). |
| **COMBINABLE — METHODOLOGICALLY SAFE** | **None.** `lib/ratings.mjs:98` bars both raters, and merging with the reference side would deliver the reference values' contested history to the scorer. As in §4, the constraint graph argues for deletion, not merger. |
| **CONTAMINATION RISK** | Structurally low and well enforced (`RATING_ITEM_KEYS` exactness, `lib/packets.mjs:16, 94-99`; §12.2 withholds rater identity, other items' labels, tallies, class, split, pilot and even **the number of disagreements**). §22 row 36 leaves "other channels PROCEDURAL". **Deleting the role deletes the channel.** |
| **INDEPENDENCE RISK** | Moderate, procedural, for zero gain in M. |
| **PLACE IN A TWO-HUMAN MODEL** | **None. Delete; replace with the pinned adverse order plus the published exclusion sensitivity.** |

**Reasoning.** Symmetrical to §4, and the same test applies: *what does the adjudicator know that the raters do not?* Read off `lib/packets.mjs:72-86`: the identical §12 rater packet, plus two labels shown unlabelled. §12.2 withholds everything else, down to the count of disagreements. So the marginal information is again **the pairwise contrast, and nothing else** — no new document, no new source, and by §12.2 no mandate to introduce a new label.

The reframing the owner should take from §4 and §7 together: **the two adjudicators are not evidence-producers; they are error-correctors that, on average, correct in the developing organisation's favour.** They exist because a disagreement would otherwise become an exclusion, and exclusions cost coverage. Replacing them with adverse resolution keeps the coverage *and* removes the favourable discretion — a strictly better position for a reader, and a strictly harder bar for the software. That is the trade the owner is actually being offered, and it should be accepted on evidentiary grounds, not on headcount grounds.

## 8. Summary

| Role | Human required? | Replacement mechanism (all pinned in `tooling_aggregate_sha256` pre-registration) | Exclusion-budget cost (of 6 at floor / 30 at cap) | Two-human place |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Enumerator | **NO** *(one empirical gate, UNVERIFIED)* | Register-snapshot frame, chain-randomised via the §5.7.1 rank key, tri-state-conservative screening | **None directly.** Indirect: a register-random frame lowers fill yield and pressures **G1**, not §15.5(2) | — |
| Reference Author | **YES** | none possible (C1 + C2 + §9.8 cap) | — | **Human #1** |
| Reference Confirmer | **YES** | none possible (same, + no second observation from blinding) | — | **Human #2** |
| Reference adjudicator | **NO** | §9.4 tier dominance; **residual** ties → `REFERENCE_CONFLICT` (a lawful §15.1 disposition) | **Low if the tier rule resolves; fatal at the floor if it does not.** Pure exclusion ≈ the whole budget at 10 % disagreement | — |
| Rater 1 | **Partly** — branch 2 only | §10.1 branches 1/3/4 + §10.2 arithmetic + temporal rule | None (mechanised branches stay VALID) | **Human #1, task 2** |
| Rater 2 | **Partly** — branch 2 only | as above; the *second instance* is irreplaceable | None | **Human #2, task 2** |
| Rating adjudicator | **NO** | adverse severity order + published exclusion sensitivity | **Zero** — resolution keeps the observation VALID. A new `RATER-DISAGREED` disposition would instead spend the budget, which is why resolution is preferred | — |

**Seven independent roles → two independent humans.** Plus the operator, who is not independent (§4A.3) and can absorb neither of the two: **three people in total.** Neither human may be an AI, and no mechanism named above is a model inference — every one is a deterministic function pinned by hash.

**Nothing above is permitted by protocol-004 as written.** §4A.3 states "**minimum 7 independent persons**" and every role's eligibility text repeats "no other §4A.3 role". Reaching two humans requires amending **§4A.2, §4A.3, §9.1, §9.2.1, §9.5, §12, §12.2** and, for the rating-adjudicator removal, either **§15.1**'s disposition list or `reconcile`/`resolve-rating-adjudication` in the tooling. No such amendment is made, drafted or proposed for adoption here; this document is analysis only.

**The two orchestrator constraints that most shape the result**, restated so they are not lost: (1) the reference and rating adjudicators are **not** symmetric — one has a lawful home for unresolved disagreement and the other does not; (2) every elimination that turns a judgement into an exclusion is spending a budget of **six observations at the floor**, which is why *resolution* rules beat *exclusion* rules throughout.
