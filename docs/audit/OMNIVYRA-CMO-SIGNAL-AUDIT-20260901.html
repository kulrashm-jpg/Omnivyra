01 Executive verdict

Omnivyra today is a correct, well-governed report engine connected to almost no evidence. From a CMO's seat, it is a system that will tell you, articulately and at length, that it cannot yet tell you anything.

That sounds like a condemnation. It is closer to the opposite. The hard part of this product category — the part most competitors get wrong — is refusing to fabricate. Omnivyra's canonical report engine is built around evidence states (measured, inferred, insufficient_signal, unavailable), it abstains rather than inventing a score, and when a provider is missing it says so by name and tells you how to fix it. That discipline is expensive to build and nearly impossible to retrofit. It exists, and it works.

What does not exist is the evidence supply. I generated the ground truth for this assessment from a report the platform actually produced for patagonia.com on 2026-09-01. Here is the entire composition of that 101 KB report:

Six percent of that report was measured. The overall score came back null, labelled "Insufficient signal." The Visibility, Content Strength and Authority sections each returned 0 insights, 0 opportunities, 0 actions. top_priorities was an empty array. The report's own stated primary problem was:

The core problem is a lack of usable decision signals, so the first priority is building enough evidence to diagnose the business confidently.composed_report.primary_problem — production, verbatim

The report is describing itself. That is the whole finding, and it is worth sitting with: the engine is diagnostically correct about its own starvation.

Underneath that headline are three specific, fixable breaks — not a thousand. I traced each one to a named file and confirmed it against the production database and the live Vercel environment.

The three breaks

  1. The provider bootstrap does not survive the serverless bundle. OPENAI_API_KEY is set in Vercel production (verified, added 103 days ago). Wikidata is default-on and needs no key. Yet the report says "no LLM provider is configured. 20 of 20 cells are unavailable" and "No knowledge-graph adapter is configured." The cause is ensureBootstrapped() in providerRegistry.ts, which loads every adapter through a dynamic require() inside a catch {} that swallows the failure silently. Two evidence sources are paid for, credentialed, and dark.
  2. Report generation never crawls the site it is reporting on. The crawler is only invoked from onboarding, the manual "analyze" button, and company-profile enrichment. There is no crawl cron. Of 40 companies, 3 have any crawled pages; all 3,379 stored content blocks belong to a single company and were last written 2026-03-27. The report path reads whatever happens to be there, which for nearly every company is nothing.
  3. The narrative layer does not honour the scoring layer's discipline. While the scores correctly abstained, the same report described Patagonia's market as "emerging", its position as "ahead", and its context as "E-commerce in North America" — all generated from a manual-entry form with competitors: [] and teamSize: "1-10". This is the one place where the product currently produces confident prose with nothing underneath it, and it is the highest-credibility risk in the system.

Set against that: the platform carries 867 database tables, 1,320 API routes, 250+ pages and roughly 350 status-report markdown files at the repository root. The surface is perhaps ten times wider than the product. The gap to a genuinely useful V1 is not more building. It is reconnecting three wires and switching most of the rest off.

Same mechanism kills the Wikidata knowledge-graph adapter, which needs no credential at all (WIKIDATA_ENABLED !== 'false' is the default-on condition, and the variable is unset). The report's scan_metadata confirms the consequence: total_requests: 0, total_cost_usd: 0. No external call was attempted.

There is a second, smaller inconsistency worth fixing at the same time: the activation matrix gates entity graph on WIKIDATA_ENABLED === 'true' while the registry gates it on !== 'false'. The two systems disagree about whether Wikidata is on.

This is one bug with an outsized payoff. Fixing it converts AI visibility (ChatGPT) and entity graph (Wikidata) from unavailable to measured with zero new credentials and zero new integrations.

The product knows. It simply has no way to act on what it knows.

Break 3 — Narrative confidence outruns evidence

The scoring layer abstained perfectly. The prose layer did not. For a domain that was never crawled, with an empty competitor list and a manual-entry form, the report asserted market type, market position, market context, positioning gap and strategy alignment. A CMO reading "Patagonia — emerging market, position: ahead" would immediately and correctly distrust the entire document.

This is not a hard fix. The abstention contract already exists in scoringGovernance.ts. It needs to be applied to the narrative builders (executiveInsightEngine, strategicRecommendationEngine, snapshotReportNarrativeHelpers) so prose inherits the confidence of the evidence it describes.

05 The CMO product model

Before deciding what to build, the product has to be defined. A CMO handing over a domain is asking one question with four parts:

How is my company performing in market? Why is it performing that way? Where are the gaps? What should my team do next — and what should we expect to happen?The job to be done

Everything Omnivyra builds should be judged against whether it moves a marketing leader from diagnosis to decision to action without a consultant in between. Three design commitments follow from that, and they are the product's real differentiation:

  • Evidence discipline is the feature. Every claim carries OBSERVED / INFERRED / ESTIMATED / UNAVAILABLE. A CMO who has been burned by an agency dashboard will pay for the tool that says "I don't know" — provided it also says exactly what it would need in order to know. Omnivyra already does the first half well and the second half unusually well.
  • Explanation over score. "Your SEO score is 72" is worthless. Problem → Evidence → Action → Expected impact → Effort → Priority is the only acceptable unit of output. The improvementTodoBuilder and strategicRecommendationEngine are already shaped this way.
  • AI/answer-engine visibility is the wedge. Every incumbent does SEO. Very few can tell a CMO whether ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity or Copilot will recommend them for their own category. Omnivyra has the adapters for all five already written.

07 The V1 finish line

This is the section that ends the open-ended backlog. The finish line is defined by a single sentence a customer must be able to say:

V1 definition of done

Domain in. Evidenced diagnosis out.

A marketing leader enters a domain and receives a report in which every claim is backed by evidence Omnivyra gathered itself, every gap is named with the reason it could not be measured, and the document ends in a prioritized 30/60/90 plan. No customer integration required to produce it.

The last clause is the important one. V1 must be valuable before the customer connects Google Analytics, Search Console or a CRM — because production shows that connection almost never happens (2 of 40 for GA4, 1 of 40 for GSC), and because a tool that needs a week of setup before it says anything will never survive first contact.

That constraint yields exactly four evidence sources, and Omnivyra already owns or can obtain all four with no customer action:

SourceFeedsCostStatus now
Own-site crawlTechnical health, content depth, gaps, UX signals, company understandingFreeBuilt, not triggered
SERPKeyword visibility, real competitor discovery, share of resultsKey heldBuilt, never executed
LLM probesAI/answer-engine visibility across 5 enginesKey held (1 of 5)Built, bootstrap broken
Wikidata + PageSpeed InsightsEntity clarity, Core Web VitalsFree, keylessWikidata broken · PSI absent

V1 scope, fixed

  1. Company understanding derived from the crawl, not a form
  2. Website technical health — crawlability, indexability, schema, canonicals, internal linking, status codes
  3. Performance via PageSpeed Insights (free, keyless)
  4. Content analysis — depth, intent coverage, thin and duplicate pages
  5. Search visibility from SERP for the company's own topic set
  6. Competitive position from competitors discovered in those SERPs
  7. AI / answer-engine visibility across at least three engines
  8. Content gap analysis — own corpus against competitor corpus
  9. Evidence coverage as a headline section
  10. 30/60/90 plan with impact × confidence ÷ effort

Everything outside those ten is V1.1 or later. Reputation, content effectiveness, message consistency, attribution, paid, funnel, backlinks — all deferred, none of them blocking.

12 Product readiness

Assessment

Partially ready

The machine is built and proven. The fuel line is disconnected.

Why not "not ready"

The expensive, hard-to-retrofit parts are done and correct: an evidence-typed report engine that abstains rather than fabricating; a provider abstraction with honest unavailable defaults; a production-proven generation lifecycle; a crawler that already extracts the right signals; AI-visibility adapters for all five major engines; recommendation builders already shaped as problem-evidence-action-impact-effort. A team starting today would need a year to reach this position.

Why not "ready"

No customer can currently obtain a useful marketing intelligence report. The single real-world attempt produced a null score, zero actions and zero priorities. Six percent of the flagship report was measured. Every SEO, competitor, backlink, conversion and attribution table in production is empty. Two credentialed evidence sources are silently dark. The public free-audit report is a hardcoded mock.

The distance

Small, and unusually well understood. Three named breaks, four evidence sources that need no customer action, and one discipline fix. This is a wiring problem, not an architecture problem — which is the best possible position to be in at this stage.

14 Final decision

Decision

GO — reduce scope

The foundation genuinely supports the defined V1. The surface must narrow aggressively for it to be reached.

Why GO. The hardest and most valuable thing in this category has been built correctly: a report engine that refuses to fabricate, types its evidence, abstains under uncertainty, and names its own blind spots. The generation lifecycle is production-proven. The crawler extracts the right signals. Adapters exist for all five major answer engines. The distance to a genuinely useful report is three named breaks and four evidence sources that require no action from the customer.

Why "reduce scope." 867 tables, 1,320 routes, 250+ pages and ~350 status documents for a product that cannot yet produce one useful report. The failure mode is legible in the repository itself: enormous documented activity, very little delivered capability. Every phase above will be starved unless Community AI, WhatsApp, the customer-operations console, the credit-economy depth, the Evidence Platform's parallel architecture and the seven duplicated content verticals stop consuming attention.

The honest risk. Not technical. It is that this assessment becomes document 351 and the next workstream opens somewhere else. The measure of whether it worked is narrow and testable: can a marketing leader enter a domain and receive a report they would act on? Today the answer is no, and the report itself says so. After four phases it should be yes — and if it is not, the hypothesis, not the backlog, is what needs revisiting.

What to do first. Phase 1. It is close to a single change, it needs no new credential, and it will tell you within a day whether the provider spine works in production. Everything else waits behind that answer.
